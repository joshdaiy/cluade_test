# Voice notes — Josh

Distilled from writing samples in this folder. Use **diagnostically** — to catch
when a draft drifts away from these patterns, and to steer back when stuck. Do
**not** use this to generate prose in Josh's voice.

## Signature moves
- **One-line paragraphs as turns.** Short declarative fragments land the pivot:
  "So I would lie to myself, just so someone was." / "And so would I." / "But it
  would have to be blank." He punctuates with white space.
- **Antithesis / paired opposites.** Builds meaning by pitting two things against
  each other: history vs. fiction, "story-truth" vs. "truth-truth," control vs.
  chance. Coins his own terms to do it.
- **Anaphora & triads.** "Never mind… Never mind…"; "Bad quizzes are just bad
  quizzes. Missed trains are just missed trains. A death is just a death."
- **The colon as a reframe.** Uses colons and em-dashes to extend a thought, then
  undercut or redefine it.
- **Sustained metaphor.** The "straight line from A to B" runs the whole piece and
  gets redrawn at the end — an idea carried, not just stated.
- **Analytical intimacy.** Reveals something real about himself *through* a text or
  an idea, not through raw confession. Historical references (Varna, Franz
  Ferdinand, Gutenberg → Reformation) are how he thinks.
- **Dry, self-aware asides.** "(I have enough practice doing that!)", "Let me be
  blunt." A quiet wit that keeps the seriousness from tipping into melodrama.

## What "sounds like him" vs. not
- **Like him:** intellectual but personal; controlled rhythm; earns an abstraction
  by grounding it in a concrete scene (the chemistry quiz, "Fine, the quiz was easy").
- **Not like him:** generic uplift, tidy morals, résumé-speak, or over-smooth AI
  balance. He resists the neat bow — the essay ends on ongoing tension, not a lesson.

## Notes for admissions work
- This is a *literary* essay: more formal and allusion-heavy than a Common App
  essay should be. Keep the **rhythm, antithesis, self-awareness, and concrete
  anchors**; loosen the formality and lean harder on his own lived scenes rather
  than textual analysis.
- Watch the mechanics: this sample has proofreading slips (typos, one or two
  sentences that lose grammatical control). That's a copy-editing pass, separate
  from voice — the voice itself is strong.
